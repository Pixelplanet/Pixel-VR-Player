## Pixel VR Player v1.11.0 — Sharp at native resolution, Prime Video & Netflix, SteamOS update fix

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Everyone must update to this version after installing the November 2026 SteamOS update
> (build 20261103 or newer).** That update replaced the system's FFmpeg 7 with FFmpeg 8,
> and every earlier Pixel VR Player release fails to start on it
> (`libavformat.so.61: cannot open shared object file`). From this release on the player
> ships its own FFmpeg, so future SteamOS updates cannot break it again.

### ✨ Sharp menus and screens — no more render-scale guessing
- The whole UI (hub, dock, menus, keyboard) and every flat video or browser screen are now
  drawn once into their own textures and placed in the world by the headset's compositor
  (OpenXR composition layers). Text and pages are crisp at the panel's native resolution,
  with none of the double resampling that used to blur them.
- The **Render Resolution** setting is gone: supersampling the eye buffers no longer
  changes how the menus or screens look, so the player always renders at native 1.0x
  (less GPU, cooler headset, same picture). 180°/360° video still renders in the eye buffers.
- Each screen's texture is sized to how big it looks from where you sit, so far-away or
  small screens (a browser in the corner) no longer shimmer.
- Screen outlines are anti-aliased.

### 🎬 Prime Video, Netflix and other streaming services
- The browser screen plays **DRM-protected video** (Widevine) — tested with Prime Video.
  Netflix, Disney+ and others use the same path. Quality is what desktop Chrome on Linux
  gets (up to 1080p).
- **Shortcuts:** *Settings › UI & Appearance › Shortcuts* lets you put YouTube, Netflix,
  Disney+, Prime Video, Twitch and the full Browser on the hub's rail — as many as you
  like; the rail makes room.
- Seeking from the dock no longer breaks a streaming service's **ad** (the film would never
  start); the timeline waits until the feature plays.

### 🪟 Frosted glass and motion
- Panels show the video behind them **blurred** through the glass (set *Panel Transparency*
  to Frosted / Glass / Clear).
- Small text uses the heavier **SemiBold** face; dock labels read "Hub", "Recenter",
  "Audio: ENG", "Curved 75%" instead of capitals.
- The hub scales in, the dock slides in, buttons and rail tabs ease on hover.
- The dock's timeline sits a little higher, clear of the button rows.

### 🧩 Multi-screen
- **Browser screens are taller** than video screens (page + toolbar); arrangements now make
  room for that instead of overlapping the row below.
- Overlapping screens no longer flicker along their seam.

### 📼 Playback
- **Chapters without names** (most disc rips, where the file only stores time marks or a
  timecode as the name) are shown as *Chapter 1, 2, …* on the timeline.
- Opening a 180°/360° clip closes the other screens **before** its decoder starts, so an
  8K clip gets the hardware decoder to itself. If another program (for example Steam's
  own web helper after it played a trailer) holds the hardware decoder, a notice names it
  instead of silently stuttering in software — restart Steam to free it.
- Browser screens get mipmaps: pages shown small no longer shimmer.

### 🛠 Under the hood
- The player bundles its own **FFmpeg 8.0** (libavcodec, libavformat, libavutil,
  libswscale, libswresample) with the zero-copy dma-buf decoder patch; the release build is
  compiled against it and no longer depends on the SteamOS FFmpeg version.
- Fixed an out-of-bounds write when allocating the video upload command buffer.
